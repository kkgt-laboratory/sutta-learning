# Sutta Of Global Knowledge

Once, the Buddha was strolling in the park where he met a group of monks who were making jokes on the gardener.
The Buddha gently went aside these monks and observed their actions. One of the monks, Anititha, who was a close
disciple of the Buddha asked him, "Buddha, does loving-friendliness have exceptions or is it without an exception
to those who are wise, and truthful?". The Buddha, gently smiled and responded, "Let us see the factors of consciousness
with dependent origination and associate only with the wise. The shroud of loving-friendliness besieges no harm on those
who benefit us, but inflicts a torment on those who doubt us and want us to be ruined. In this stage of metta, we see
that the boundless knowledge that metta provides us, does not just protect us, but keeps us in a state of judgemental
enlightenment.".

To this, the monks felt surprised and asked the Buddha once again, "Buddha, does the mind deceive us when we say
that metta is a boundless feeling that knows no surprises but only the state where one sees Nibbana in clear
daylight?". The Buddha, was subtly shocked by this question, and gently responded, "Monks, the mind is luminous,
and when the light of enlightenment permeates consciousness, it makes no distinction between those who are greedy
and lustful, and those who are kind and wise. In the same accord, the gentleness and directness of Nibbana wants us
to know the unconditioned as a direct undertaking of our daily lives, and brings only clarity but also discernment
and clear comprehension which helps us distinguish between the wise and unskillful.".

The monks now understood the explanation that Buddha provided and instantly stopped making jokes on the gardener.
They approached the gardener and greeted him with sweets and delicacies. To this, the Buddha said, "Loving-friendliness
does not mean treating your friends with compassion every single day, but being a single embodied force of wisdom
that transcends the non-unity that the mind brings when the discriminative mind opposes a singular action. This is
how we realize that our role in the Dhamma is not limited to the light of clear comprehension but the efflusive force
of discernment that helps us know what is the right action in a given moment or situation.".

The monks gradually understood their mistake, and brought forth their right action to the given moment. They spoke
to the gardener, and some monks helped the gardener perform his duties for the Dhamma. As we understand this story,
we see that loving-friendliness doesn't just mean unlawful compassion, but a focused stream of awareness which
guides the chitta and brings skillfulness to the daily duties of the discriminative mind.
