# Sutta Of Unification

Once the Buddha was asked by several senior monks, a simple question "What happens when the mind gets unified and how does right concentration help
attain higher states of Nibbana that are known only in Jhana.". While the Buddha was sipping a cup of tea, he gently strolled down the hallway and
asked the monks to follow him as they seeked the answer to their question.

The Buddha responded, "O Monks, the mind is fettered and degenerated by forces that are known to hold humanity in a state of captivity.
When this desolate power of the mind is chained to samsara, only defilements, fetters, and hindrances become a cloud in judgement, and
cause a reception of negativity. With right concentration, the mind becomes unified, and the fetters dissolve into the bright, luminous force
of the chitta. On this arising, all traumas, pains, and negative feelings dissolve into the void, and the mind becomes nourished. This
nourishment, when peppered with loving-friendliness leads to higher states of Nibbana that are accessible without the practice of Jhana."

The monks were pleasantly surprised because the held the opinion that only through the practice of Jhana, one can progress through the higher
stages of Nibbana and without Jhana there is no growth when the mind is free from samsara but still chained by greed, hatred, and delusion. On
this note, a fellow monk Sutipanna asked the Buddha; "Dearest Buddha, when the mind is still chained by greed, hatred, and delusion, and there's
no presence of Jhana, is it true that the force of loving-friendliness permeates the light of wisdom and frees the self from agony?".

The Buddha smiled and responded, "Yes, Sutipanna, loving-friendliness has no bounds or barriers and when one truly experiences the force of metta
in it's trueest sense, the mind unfolds into a state of unlimited wisdom and equanimity which can be surpassing of the tide of Jhanic awareness.
This is not a desolate goal, but the goal of true nobility where even in conditions where concentration is sparse, right concentration can be
cultivated, and the mind can attained unconditioned states of being in a continuous and mobile manner.".

The monks were delighted by the Buddha's response and proceeded to the Dhamma hall for their light meal and Dhamma talk. The Buddha proceeded
with the monks to the Dhamma hall and continued on the expedition of knowing the mind in it's entirety despite being fully enlightened.

**Understanding:**

It is a common misconception that Jhana is necessary to attain full enlightenment. In this sutta, the Buddha basks on the fact that
even without Jhana and a strong practice of loving-friendliness (metta), the path of Nibbana can be completely traversed and known
to the point where it does not seem to be a path anymore, but a continuous outpouring of compassion, wisdom, equanimity, and mindfulness.
When this is completely understood, the grip of ignorance reaches a point of cessation, and the chitta burns brightly to invoke our true self.

On a similar note, Jhana is necessary to evolve in the practice of meditation, but in situations where cultivation of Jhana is not possible,
the Buddha points to the system of awareness that transcends tranquility, and concentration to show that even simple practise of cultivating
gratitude, morality, wholesomeness, and loving-friendliness helps us receive the higher stages of Jhana through a continous movement of metta.
