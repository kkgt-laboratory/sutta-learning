# The Discourse on Loving-Friendliness and Wisdom (Mettāpaññā Sutta)

**Thus have I heard.**

At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Jetavana, in the monastery of Anāthapiṇḍika near Sāvatthī. During that time, several monks were speaking mockingly and making jokes about a gardener who worked within the monastery grounds.

Then the Venerable Anitittha approached the Blessed One. Having paid homage, he sat respectfully to one side and said:

"Venerable Sir, is loving-friendliness limited in its scope, or is it truly boundless? How should a monk dwell so that loving-friendliness and wisdom are both fully developed?"

The Blessed One replied:

"Monk, loving-friendliness is not accompanied by hostility. Boundless indeed is the liberation of mind through loving-friendliness. Yet one who develops loving-friendliness without understanding the nature of experience has not completed the path.

When a practitioner understands contact, understands the arising of contact, understands the cessation of contact, and understands the path leading to the cessation of contact, then loving-friendliness and wisdom grow together.

Dependent upon contact arises feeling. Dependent upon feeling arises craving. When one carefully contemplates these processes with wise attention, craving gradually fades away. When craving ceases, the mind becomes liberated."

The monks then asked:

"Blessed One, how does a mind of loving-friendliness embrace all beings equally?"

The Blessed One answered:

"Monks, this mind is luminous by nature, but it is obscured by adventitious defilements. When greed is abandoned, when hatred is abandoned, and when delusion is abandoned, the mind shines with its natural radiance.

Such a practitioner does not reserve loving-friendliness only for friends, nor does he cultivate hostility toward those who oppose him. Rather, he dwells with a heart seeking the welfare of all beings. He cultivates compassion, develops sympathetic joy, and abides in equanimity."

Hearing these words, the monks reflected deeply upon their conduct. They approached the gardener with minds filled with goodwill and friendliness. Some assisted him in his work, while others spoke kindly and respectfully.

Seeing this transformation, the Blessed One addressed the monks once more:

"Monks, loving-friendliness is not merely pleasant speech or outward courtesy. Loving-friendliness cultivated together with wisdom becomes the root of wholesome conduct.

Where loving-friendliness and wisdom meet, Right Speech is fulfilled. Right Action is fulfilled. Right Livelihood is fulfilled. Such a person acts not from selfish desire, but from understanding and compassion.

Therefore, monks, you should train yourselves in this way:

'We shall dwell with minds of loving-friendliness. We shall view the world through the eye of wisdom. With immeasurable hearts, we shall extend goodwill toward all beings without exception.'

This is how you should train yourselves."

Thus spoke the Blessed One. Delighted in the Blessed One's words, the monks rejoiced and put his teaching into practice.

---

### Core Teaching

This discourse teaches that **loving-friendliness (mettā) and wisdom (paññā) must develop together**. Loving-friendliness without wisdom can become attachment or sentimentality, while wisdom without loving-friendliness can become cold and detached. Through understanding dependent origination, abandoning greed, hatred, and delusion, and cultivating the Four Divine Abodes—loving-friendliness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity—the practitioner gradually purifies the mind and walks the path toward liberation and Nibbāna.
