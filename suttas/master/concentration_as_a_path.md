# Concentration as a Path

When the mind is in a state of ultrafication, the sense gates do not recede completely till there is enough evidence that security
is bound and boundless to the state of the field that permeates absoluteness. This is a retrospection on the rememberence in the mind's
chambers also classified in terms of sub-minds where all 3; peripheral awareness, introspective awareness and metacognitive introspective awareness
work together to sortify the strands of the self, and bring consciousness to a state of ultimate normalcy. When we develop concentration,
and only concentration, all the complementary skills of mindfulness such as sensory clarity, clarity, zeropoint, equanimity, gratitude,
and even loving-friendliness, develop, and mature to make insight experiences a complete, total, unified, and unfixated experience of reality.

Hence, concentration (samatha-samadhi) by itself is a valid path to enlightenment, because it promotes ultrafication. The meaning of ultrafication
is profound because it simply means that the strands of the self join and rejoin multiple times, to point to the ultimate or ultreme truth in reality
which is just one; "Nibbana is a total, boundless experience that has no beginning and end, whristwhile being unconditioned and impermanently permanent".
Ultrafication is a natural consequence, an effect of strong and right concentration because it helps grow the muscles of mindfulness in all factors,
which may be 118, 514, 1021, or 5087 depending on how we square out the map of consciousness, enlightenment, wisdom, and pannivardhana or insightful wisdom.
The most basic concomitant of right concentration is ultrafication, and ultrafication comes from right concentration and strong concentration which
is not a piece-meal treatment, but a unified source of compassion, goodwill, tranquility, equanimity, sympathetic joy, and forcesis.

The further we expand upon the path of right concentration, we see that ultrafication may never bring unwholesome qualities because it is boundless,
and is characterized as the "factor of nibbana" making us captulated, coverted, concised, and contained within the shoonya (void) in a holistic,
purposeful, boundless, characterized, and factored notion of the self that is not covered in the standard practise of sati-samadhi. Monks often
confuse ultrafication for unification because it is complete, and polymaxical in nature, however, ultrafication deserves it's own unique treatment
as attention and awareness converge onto a global field of awareness that is self-intelligent, and self-regenerative in it's due nature.

We must not hold ourselves accountable for the effects of ultrafication which is a natural feature in "concentration as a path" but make it
well integrated, and completely coaelesced within our incorporation of the self, which continually evolves and becomes charged with the
flun of chitta and the soaring heat of meditation's purifying flame of wisdom that burns the defilements, fetters, and hindrances repeatedly
till there is no scope for a non-doing activity of greed, hatred, delusion, and lust to become a reality in the sphere of all streams of consciousness.
This system of practice requires training not just in terms of virtue, morality, ethics, and honesty which we group as "sila" but also
the structured training of awareness to magnify and miopon the source of power that we produce within ourselves as a yogi, mindfulness practitioner,
or as an enlightened being seeking to uncover the light of wisdom for better living and harmonious flourishing.

The mind naturally outgrows itself, and insight digestions itself may bring upon 918 factors / aspects of pain when ultrafication reaches it's peak,
leading to sensations that may seem like fetters, but in reality, are just the body's coping mechanism to handle the vastness of shoonya (void). As we
keep proceeding to higher stages of the 7 pillars; right concentration, ultrafication, unfixation, unification, embodiment, ekagratha (onefoldness),
and trifectaness, we become seemingly free from samsaric shackles that may make us see, hear, and feel a non-dual sense of entitlement towards
ourselves, thus and forthcomingly clarified by instantaneous digestion of insight experiences, wisdom, feelingness, and kindness. Concentration
as a path is one of the most direct approaches to growing, scaling, preceeding, and directing the path of Nibbana that makes us unified with
the goals of the Dhamma and perpetually righted in a stateless state of Nibbanic Sashodhana or unconditioned awakenings.
