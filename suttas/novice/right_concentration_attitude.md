**Anuruddha Sutta: The Distinguishment of Concentration**

Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the Blessed One was dwelling at Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika's park. The Venerable Anuruddha approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. The Venerable Anuruddha said:

"Venerable Sir, it is said that concentration is a requisite for the realization of Nibbāna. However, I have come to understand that there is a distinction to be made between right concentration and wrong concentration. Could the Blessed One please expound upon this distinction, and how it relates to the development of compassion and attitude?"

The Blessed One replied: "Anuruddha, there are indeed two types of concentration: right concentration and wrong concentration. Right concentration is developed through the cultivation of the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right exertions, the four bases of psychic power, the five spiritual faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, and the eightfold path. This type of concentration is accompanied by wisdom, morality, and the development of compassion and loving-kindness.

"On the other hand, wrong concentration is developed through the cultivation of concentration without the accompaniment of wisdom and morality. This type of concentration can lead to the development of a rigid and inflexible mind, devoid of compassion and loving-kindness. One who practices wrong concentration may become indifferent to the suffering of others, and may even develop a sense of disdain or superiority over those who do not share their concentration.

"Anuruddha, an example of wrong concentration is the concentration developed by those who seek to attain mystical powers or to gain control over others. This type of concentration is not accompanied by wisdom or compassion, and can lead to harm and suffering for oneself and others.

"Right concentration, on the other hand, is developed with the intention of realizing Nibbāna, and is accompanied by the development of compassion, loving-kindness, and wisdom. One who practices right concentration will cultivate a mind that is flexible, open, and receptive to the suffering of others. They will develop a sense of empathy and understanding, and will seek to alleviate the suffering of all beings.

"Anuruddha, the distinction between right concentration and wrong concentration is a crucial one. Wrong concentration can lead to a narrow and limited understanding of the world, whereas right concentration can lead to a deep and profound understanding of the nature of reality. One who practices right concentration will come to understand the impermanent, suffering, and non-self nature of all phenomena, and will develop a sense of detachment and dispassion towards all things.

"Ultimately, Anuruddha, the practice of right concentration is essential for the realization of Nibbāna. It is through the development of right concentration that one can gain a direct understanding of the effects of wrong concentration on compassion and attitude. One will come to see how wrong concentration can lead to a rigid and inflexible mind, devoid of compassion and loving-kindness, and how right concentration can lead to a mind that is flexible, open, and receptive to the suffering of others.

"Thus, Anuruddha, I encourage you to cultivate right concentration, accompanied by wisdom, morality, and the development of compassion and loving-kindness. May you come to understand the distinction between right concentration and wrong concentration, and may you realize the profound benefits of practicing right concentration for the attainment of Nibbāna."

**Understanding:**

When we understand right concentration, the effects of wrong concentration are directly reflected upon by strong mindfulness. Right concentration is strong, one-pointed, and penetrating conscious concentration whereas wrong concentration is shallow, limited, and restrained to false understanding of the self with a clear lack of clear comprehension. This is explained by the Buddha in this sutta while demonstrating how right concentration is used to unfold the contents of the mind and shed light through the chitta on all wordly and other worldy knowings.

Right concentration is packed with loving-friendliness, compassion, morality, integrity, ethics, wisdom, equanimity, sensory clarity, clear comprehension and a complete and total, mindful knowing of the Dhamma. Right concentration helps you understand the Dhamma totally, and once the Dhamma is totally understood, only then Nibanna is attained. On the other hand, wrong concentration strays you away from the path of Nibbana, and makes mindfulness weaker in a parallel stream to the way sati-samadhi and samatha-vipassana is practised and understood.
