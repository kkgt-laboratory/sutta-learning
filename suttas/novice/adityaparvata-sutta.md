**Adityaparvata Sutta**

Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the Buddha was dwelling near a hill crest, where the sky unfolded its majestic hues at sunrise and sunset. The Buddha rose early, before the break of day, and stood at the crest, his feet planted firmly on the earth. As the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, he began to walk, his footsteps deliberate and mindful.

As he walked, the Buddha chanted softly to himself, "Buddho, Buddho," reminding himself of the awakened state that lies beyond the cycles of samsara. With each step, he cultivated the right attitude, acknowledging the impermanent and ephemeral nature of all phenomena. His feet touched the earth, and he felt the energy of the body, the unity of breath and movement, as he surrendered to the present moment.

The Buddha's disciples, gathered around him, observed his mindful footsteps and inquired about the significance of this practice. The Buddha replied, "My dear friends, as I walk near this hill crest, I am reminded of the cyclical nature of samsara. The sun rises and sets, only to rise again, in an endless cycle. So too, the conditioned mind rises and falls, bound by the chains of ignorance and craving.

"But the mindful cultivation of the Buddho, the awakened state, allows us to transcend this cycle. By settling the breath, unifying the body and energy, and embracing the present moment, we can break free from the conditioned mind and realize the unconditioned freedom that lies beyond.

"Thus, I walk with mindfulness, cultivating the right attitude and the right command of the cyclical nature of samsara. May you, too, my dear friends, walk with mindfulness, and may the chant of Buddho guide you on your journey toward the unconditioned state, where the cycles of birth and death are transcended, and the radiant light of wisdom shines forth."

And so, the Buddha's disciples practiced the mindful cultivation of the Buddho, walking with deliberate footsteps, and chanting the sacred syllable, as they, too, sought to transcend the cycles of samsara and realize the unconditioned freedom that lies beyond.

**Understanding:**

When we practise walking meditation, it's recommended to chant "Buddho" repeatedly instead of attending to the fleeting and enormous nature of thoughts or dome calls. Gradually, with time, the energy of mindfulness envelopes our being, as it does normally, during walking meditation and we feel a sense of peace and calm. Moreover, the cyclic nature of Samsara and wordly activities turns to cessation, and we move from a state of suffering and pain to a direct realization that nature is not limited for the unconditioned.

The Buddha also mentions the cyclic nature of Samsaric suffering. Enlightened beings take on samsara for various purposes during their lifetime, and due to this, their mind and energy becomes conditioned and chained. The Buddha highlights that several forces in Nature operate on a cyclic theme simply because Nature demands it, but not because the being that operates the theme or force requires it. For instance, the sun rises in a cyclic nature and sets in a cyclic nature, but the forces that keep the sun in this state of cyclic nature are unconditioned and governed by Nibanna.
