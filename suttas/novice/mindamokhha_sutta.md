**Mindahamokkha Sutta**

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Savatthi in Jeta's Grove, Anathapindika's Park. There, the Blessed One addressed the monks: "Monks, the mind is like a functor, a function that maps the complexities of the self onto the terrain of reality. Just as a functor clarifies the strings of a mathematical equation, the mind can clarify the strings of the self that hold the mindful monk in a state of agony, mishappenings, and fear.

"These strings, monks, are like the knots that bind a rope, constricting the flow of wisdom and compassions. They are born from the interactions of craving, aversion, and delusion, and they manifest as the thoughts, emotions, and perceptions that cloud the mind.

"When the mind is entangled in these strings, monks, it loses its clarity and focus, and the monk becomes mired in a state of suffering and discontent. The mindful monk, however, recognizes these strings and sets out to untangle them, using the sword of wisdom to cut through the knots of ignorance.

"But, monks, this is not a simple task. The mind is a cunning foe, and it will stop at nothing to maintain its grip on the self. It will conjure up all manner of fears, doubts, and distractions to keep the monk from attaining meditative enlightenment and tranquility.

"Fear, in particular, is a formidable obstacle, monks. It is like a dark cloud that shrouds the mind, casting a shadow over the landscape of the self. Fear of loss, fear of gain, fear of pleasure, fear of pain - all these fears and more will arise to challenge the monk's resolve and determination.

"And yet, monks, it is precisely in the face of fear that the mindful monk finds the greatest opportunity for growth and liberation. For when the mind is confronted with fear, it is forced to confront its own limitations and vulnerabilities. It is forced to recognize the impermanence and uncertainty of all things, and to let go of its attachment to the self.

"In this way, monks, the mindful monk comes to understand that the self is not a fixed entity, but a fluid and ever-changing process. The self is like a river, constantly flowing and shifting, and it is only by embracing this flux that the monk can find true peace and liberation.

"Thus, monks, I urge you to cultivate mindfulness and wisdom, to develop the clarity and focus that will allow you to navigate the complexities of the self and to untangle the strings that bind you. May you find the courage to confront your fears and to let go of your attachments, and may you attain the meditative enlightenment and tranquility that is your birthright."

**Understanding:**

The energy of mindfulness helps us see that the self is an ever-changing continuous process that responds and behaves like a functor, which is a device that transforms energy from one form to another, thus showing us that the 5 aggregates; form, feeling, perception, mental formations and consciousness build the strings or strands of the self. When the monk is damned by fear, or by attachment, there is a loss of integrity in the factor associated with wisdom, insight, compassion, and clear comprehension making the body and energy of mindfulness unbalanced.

When we understand this sutta with clear comprehension, we see that our right intention and right thoughts help overcome feelings of greed, hatred, delusion, and lust and push the mind in the direction of awakening and Nibbana rather than suffering and bondage. With this, we know that only right mindfulness and right concentration when working together, can lead to freedom and liberation and all distractions are merely expansions of the poisons that cloud the true nature or true judgement of the mind.
