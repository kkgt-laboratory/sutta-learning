**Matughata Sutta**

Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the Blessed One was dwelling at Savatthi in Jeta's Grove, Anathapindika's Park. There, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhunis:

"Bhikkhunis, just as a mother desires the well-being and happiness of her children, so too should you strive to cultivate mindfulness and wisdom in order to bring peace and tranquility to those who are affected by the suffering of womb poisoning.

"This suffering, bhikkhunis, is like a dark cloud that overshadows the mind, bringing distress and discomfort to the body. But just as the sun breaks through the clouds, illuminating all that is around it, so too can the light of mindfulness and wisdom dispel the darkness of suffering.

"To cultivate this mindfulness, bhikkhunis, sit comfortably with your eyes closed and focus your attention on the breath. As you inhale, imagine that you are breathing in peace and tranquility. As you exhale, imagine that you are breathing out any feelings of distress or discomfort.

"As you practice this mindfulness, bhikkhunis, reflect on the impermanent nature of all phenomena. Recognize that suffering arises and passes away, like the ebb and flow of the tides. Understand that it is not a permanent or inherent part of the self, but rather a fleeting experience that can be transformed through the cultivation of wisdom.

"Direct your attention to the present moment, bhikkhunis, and observe the sensations in the body without judgment or attachment. As you do so, you will begin to develop a sense of detachment and equanimity, like a lotus flower that rises above the muddy waters, untouched by the impurities that surround it.

"In this way, bhikkhunis, you will come to realize that the suffering of womb poisoning is not a fixed or inherent part of the self, but rather a condition that can be transformed through the application of mindfulness and wisdom. As you cultivate this understanding, you will begin to experience a sense of peace and tranquility that is not dependent on external circumstances, but rather arises from the depths of your own heart.

"May all beings, bhikkhunis, be free from the suffering of womb poisoning. May they cultiviate mindfulness and wisdom, and may they experience the peace and tranquility that arises from the direct application of panna and sati. May they be happy, may they be healthy, and may they be at peace."

The bhikkhunis, delighted in the words of the Blessed One, rejoiced and applied themselves to the practice of mindfulness and wisdom, bringing peace and tranquility to all those who were affected by the suffering of womb poisoning.
