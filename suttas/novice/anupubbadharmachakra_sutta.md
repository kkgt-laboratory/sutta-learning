**Anupubbadharmachakka Sutta**

Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the Blessed One was dwelling at Savatthi, in the Jeta's Grove, Anathapindika's Park. There, the Blessed One addressed the monks:

"Monks, the cycle of Dhamma is not to be confused with the cycle of samsara. Samsara is the wheel of suffering, driven by ignorance and craving. But the cycle of Dhamma is a direct insight into the awakening of the Buddha, as we understand it through the Noble Eightfold Path.

"Just as a wheel turns around its axle, the cycle of Dhamma turns around the axle of wisdom. The spokes of this wheel are the eight factors of the path: right understanding, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration.

"As the wheel of Dhamma turns, it cuts through the bonds of ignorance and craving, revealing the truth of the present moment. It is a cycle of awakening, not a cycle of suffering. With each turn of the wheel, the mind becomes clearer, more focused, and more at peace.

"The cycle of Dhamma is not something that can be seen with the physical eye, but it can be known through direct experience. It is a cycle of transformation, where the unwholesome is abandoned and the wholesome is developed. It is a cycle of liberation, where the mind is freed from the bonds of suffering.

"Monks, I teach you this cycle of Dhamma, that you may understand the true nature of the path and may walk it with clarity and confidence. May you turn the wheel of Dhamma in your own hearts, and may you realize the awakened state of the Buddha, free from the cycle of suffering and rebirth."

The monks, having heard the Blessed One's words, were filled with joy and gratitude. They bowed down to the Blessed One, and departed, determined to turn the wheel of Dhamma in their own hearts, and to realize the awakened state of the Buddha.
