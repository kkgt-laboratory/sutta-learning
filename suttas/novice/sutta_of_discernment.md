**Sutta of Discerning Associations**

Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the Blessed One was dwelling at Savatthi, in the Jetavana Grove. Then, a certain monk approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. The monk asked the Blessed One:

"Venerable sir, how do we discern between wrong people and right people in the Dhamma, so that we may associate with those who will bring us benefit and avoid those who will bring us harm?"

The Blessed One said: "Monk, a person who is endowed with right concentration, equanimity, wisdom, mindful tolerance, compassion, and gratitude will discern between wrong people and right people in the Dhamma.

"A wrong person in the Dhamma is one who is devoid of right concentration, and thus their mind is distracted and unstable. They are lacking in equanimity, and thus they are swayed by emotions and prejudices. They are unwise, and thus they do not understand the true nature of reality. They are intolerant, and thus they are quick to anger and resentment. They are without compassion, and thus they do not care for the well-being of others. They are ungrateful, and thus they do not appreciate the good that has been done for them.

"On the other hand, a right person in the Dhamma is one who is endowed with right concentration, and thus their mind is stable and focused. They are endowed with equanimity, and thus they are not swayed by emotions and prejudices. They are wise, and thus they understand the true nature of reality. They are tolerant, and thus they are patient and understanding. They are compassionate, and thus they care for the well-being of others. They are grateful, and thus they appreciate the good that has been done for them.

"Monk, when you associate with a right person in the Dhamma, you will find that they will bring you benefit and help you to realize the knowings of reality. They will teach you the Dhamma, and guide you on the path to enlightenment. They will be a source of comfort and support, and will help you to overcome the obstacles that you may encounter on your journey.

"But when you associate with a wrong person in the Dhamma, you will find that they will bring you harm and hinder your progress on the path. They will teach you wrong views, and lead you astray from the true path. They will be a source of discomfort and distress, and will increase the obstacles that you may encounter on your journey.

"Therefore, monk, it is essential that you cultivate right concentration, equanimity, wisdom, mindful tolerance, compassion, and gratitude, so that you may discern between wrong people and right people in the Dhamma. In this way, you will be able to associate with those who will bring you benefit, and avoid those who will bring you harm. And by doing so, you will realize the knowings of reality, and attain the ultimate goal of the holy life."

The monk was satisfied with the Blessed One's answer, and rose from his seat, paid homage to the Blessed One, and departed.
