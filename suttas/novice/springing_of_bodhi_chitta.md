**Anuruddha Sutta: The Springing of Bodhi Chitta**

Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the Blessed One was dwelling at the Jetavana monastery, near the city of Savatthi. The Venerable Anuruddha approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. He then asked the Blessed One:

"Venerable sir, how does the nature of wisdom apply to understanding the Dhamma, and what is the sign of Bodhi chitta springing from the wells of the unconscious to bring about the direct emancipation from greed, hatred, delusion, and lust?"

The Blessed One replied: "Anuruddha, the nature of wisdom is to see things as they truly are. It is the ability to discern the impermanent, suffering, and non-self nature of all phenomena. When this wisdom is applied to understanding the Dhamma, it reveals the true nature of reality and cuts through the chains and shackles of ignorance and doubt.

"The sign of Bodhi chitta springing from the wells of the unconscious is the arising of mindfulness, clear comprehension, and a sense of detachment. As the mind becomes more aware of its own workings, the Bhikkhu begins to see the impermanent and suffering nature of all phenomena, including his own thoughts, feelings, and perceptions.

"With this understanding, the Bhikkhu can begin to let go of greed, hatred, delusion, and lust. He sees that these defilements are the root causes of suffering and that they are based on a fundamental misconception of the nature of reality. As he lets go of these defilements, the Bhikkhu rises above the chains and shackles of ignorance and doubt, and he begins to experience a sense of freedom and liberation.

"This freedom and liberation are not the result of any external cause, but rather they arise from the direct understanding of the Dhamma. The Bhikkhu sees that all phenomena are empty of inherent existence and that they are mere conceptual constructs. This understanding brings about a sense of peace, tranquility, and detachment, which is the hallmark of the enlightened mind.

"Anuruddha, the Bhikkhu who has developed this understanding is like a lotus flower that grows in the mud but remains unsoiled by it. He is like a bird that flies through the sky but leaves no trail behind. He is like a wise man who has crossed the ocean of suffering and has reached the shore of liberation.

"Therefore, Anuruddha, I exhort you to develop this understanding, to cultivate this wisdom, and to apply it to your daily life. May you rise above the chains and shackles of ignorance and doubt, and may you experience the direct emancipation from greed, hatred, delusion, and lust. May you attain the peace, tranquility, and detachment that is the hallmark of the enlightened mind."

**Understanding:**

The nature of greed, hatred, delusion and lust is by itself, the cause and preponent of pain and suffering in human life. When we expand upon the source of wisdom, and gratitude we realise that greed, hatred, delusion and lust brings ignorance which in turn brings contact onto unwholesome factors to further the grip of hindrances, fetters, and defilements. By this understanding of dependent origination, we see that the mind is inherently pure and only through means of right concentration which surfaces as enlightened wisdom, the chains of ignorance can be lifted which naturally eliminates the source of pain and suffering in human life.
